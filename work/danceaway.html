01

Where I started

Role, team & constraints

The core product challenge: people in the dance ecosystem were scattered across disconnected platforms — making it hard to be discovered, build reputation, or find the right opportunities. DanceAway needed to turn that fragmented world into a structured mobile experience where different roles could connect around real needs.

Understanding the environment explains the scope of what I needed to build. I worked across product, research, UI, brand, content, and handoff — because the product was still being defined and the team was small.

My role & team
  • Role: Lead Product Designer
  • Team: Founder · Developer · Content team · External collaborators
  • Stage: Pre-launch / early-stage startup
  • Reporting: No design manager — self-directed
Constraints I worked within
  • No prior UX process or design documentation
  • No design manager or feedback structure
  • Fast iteration with limited QA resources
  • Shifting product scope as research evolved
  • Responsible for research, design, and handoff simultaneously

These constraints are why I built systems, not just deliverables.

04

Product thinking

When I saw what the brief missed

My mentor advised starting with proto-personas — a standard approach. But as I mapped the ecosystem, I noticed something: we were only designing for dancers. Organizers, studios, and recruiters were completely invisible in our model.

→
Original scope
Dancers only. One user type. One research focus.
!
Risk I identified
Organizers, studios, recruiters, and event promoters were absent from the model — but would shape how the product grows and monetizes.
✓
My intervention
Mapped the full dance ecosystem in parallel: all roles, their interactions, and what each segment needed from the product.
↑
Product impact
Changed personas, onboarding logic, post types, and surfaced monetization paths we hadn't seen before.

"This is the difference between executing a brief and owning a product."

05

Core decision

Bio → structured tags

Research insight → UX decision → scalable product value. One screen redesign that changed the product's ability to create discovery.

Before
Write your bio
I've been dancing bachata for 6 years. I love connection and musicality in social dancing. I also teach beginners.
  • Bio is unsearchable — data buried in free text
  • No way to filter or match by style or focus
  • Users don't know what to write
  • Platform can't extract structured signals
→
After
What makes you unique?
We'll help turn your bio into searchable profile signals
Bachata Connection Musicality Beginner Teacher
  • Profile searchable by role, style & focus
  • Structured data enables better recommendations
  • Reduces friction — voice input & auto-suggestions
  • Foundation for discovery, matching & monetization
06

Product impact

How the insight changed the product

One research pattern rippled through the entire product structure.

01
Expanded the user model
Moved from 'dancers only' to full ecosystem: organizers, studios, recruiters, event promoters.
02
Bio → structured profile tags
Turned free-text bio into searchable signals. Foundation for discovery, matching, and future monetization.
03
Separated account creation from profile setup
Split onboarding into intent-first steps to reduce cognitive load and collect better data at each stage.
04
Content types in feed
Defined distinct logic for Article, Event, Job, Huddle, Social Post, Merch, Venue — separate goals, layouts, permissions.
05
Persona-based post creation
Post creation logic varies by account type, reducing cognitive load and irrelevant options per role.
06
Discovery & matching foundation
Structured tags created the data layer needed for search, recommendations, and future matching logic.

Research

37-platform competitive audit

Not a UI teardown — a business intelligence exercise. 37 platforms across 6 market segments, analyzed for positioning, feature gaps, monetization models, UX friction, and tone of voice.

01
Dance Learning
Curriculum, video-led, skill progression
02
Events & Ticketing
Discovery, booking flows, organizer tools
03
Talent Marketplaces
Hiring, portfolio, recruiter-dancer match
04
Studio Management
Scheduling, retention, business tools
05
Social & Community
Short video, discovery, dance-adjacent
06
Fitness & Lifestyle
Indirect competitors, overlapping intent

Output: strategic differentiation brief — what no competitor was doing, and where DanceAway could own a unique position.
